## Relevance to HYP-004

This paper is the most direct empirical grounding for HYP-004's entropy-labeling mechanism. Kadavath et al. define H(A|Q) = E_A[-log P(A|Q)] over T=1 samples and show it discriminates correct vs. incorrect answers across TriviaQA, Arithmetic, Lambda, and HumanEval. However, the critical finding is that **on HumanEval, AUROC for entropy-based discrimination decreases with model size**, because large models can generate many *diverse correct* solutions. This is exactly the "conflating uncertainty with difficulty" risk called out in HYP-004.

## Key mechanism detail

The paper also explores "stuffing" 30 sampled answers into a single context and measuring cross-sample loss as a proxy for diversity—a cheaper variant of the k-sample entropy approach proposed in HYP-004. Results are mixed: TriviaQA benefits, HumanEval does not, for the same reason.

## Implication for HYP-004 design

HYP-004 normalizes entropy by mean response length, which partially addresses one confound. But the model-capability confound (larger models = more creative-yet-correct code) suggests that on code tasks, the entropy labeler may systematically overestimate difficulty and route too many coding queries to Opus.

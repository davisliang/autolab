## Hypothesis HYP-001: Multi-Signal GBDT Complexity Router

### Claim
A gradient-boosted decision tree (GBDT) router trained on a rich feature vector derived from the query — including token length, lexical diversity (type-token ratio), syntactic parse depth, unigram perplexity under a small language model, named-entity density, and a pooled sentence-embedding — achieves ≥85% quality retention relative to always-routing-to-large, while simultaneously routing ≥40% of queries away from the large model to cheaper tiers. This multi-signal approach outperforms single-feature baselines (length-only, embedding-only).

### Motivation
Existing routing systems (FrugalGPT, LLM-Blender, RouteLLM) rely on either simple heuristics or a single embedding similarity score. They miss complementary signals: a short query can be deeply technical (high perplexity) while a long query can be boilerplate. Combining signals with a GBDT captures nonlinear interactions and generalizes across task types.

### Experimental Setup
- **Dataset**: Stratified sample of MMLU, GSM8K, MT-Bench, and HellaSwag — representing reasoning, math, multi-turn, and commonsense tasks. Use pre-cached model responses from small/medium/large open-source proxy models (Llama-3.2-1B / Llama-3.1-8B / Llama-3.1-70B) to stand in for Haiku/Sonnet/Opus.
- **Router**: XGBoost trained on 6 features per query; labels derived from which tier achieves acceptable quality (≥95% of large-model score).
- **Evaluation**: Quality = weighted average normalized task score. Cost = per-query FLOPs proxy. Compare to always-large, always-small, random routing, length-threshold baseline.

### Pre-registered Prediction
- **metric**: `quality_retention_at_40pct_cost_reduction` — quality score of the routed system divided by the always-large quality score, evaluated at the operating point where ≥40% of queries go to a cheaper tier.
- **threshold**: 0.85
- **direction**: greater (we claim ≥0.85)

### Why This Is Novel
Prior work uses single-signal routers. This hypothesis tests whether multi-signal fusion consistently improves the quality-cost Pareto frontier, specifically the tail case where short-but-hard queries fool single-signal routers.

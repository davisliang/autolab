## Speculative Sampling (Chen, Borgeaud, Irving et al., DeepMind 2023) — Relevance to HYP-003

Concurrent and independent discovery of the same speculative execution principle as Leviathan et al. (2211.17192). Validates HYP-003's core borrowed mechanism at 70B scale in distributed serving.

### What it does
- **Draft model** p(·|·) generates K token drafts autoregressively.
- **Target model** q(·|·) scores K+1 distributions in one parallel call.
- Modified rejection sampling: accept/reject each draft token left-to-right, recovering exact target distribution.

### Key results
- **2–2.5× speedup** on Chinchilla (70B) in distributed multi-TPU serving setup.
- Throughput often exceeds the theoretical ceiling of autoregressive sampling speed.
- The scheme handles the distributed communication overhead that pure autoregressive decoding compounds.

### Significance for HYP-003
Two independent research groups simultaneously discovered that firing a cheap computation speculatively (token draft / Haiku call) while the expensive computation runs can yield significant wall-clock gains — and both proved it formally. The fact that neither paper applied this principle to the routing decision (which LLM to call) rather than the token sequence itself is precisely the gap HYP-003 exploits.

Acceptance rate for speculative decoding is typically 70–85% for well-matched draft/target pairs. HYP-003 predicts a Haiku routing accuracy of ~70%, which maps to a similar regime.

## Summary

Xiong et al. (2024) systematically benchmark uncertainty elicitation methods across GPT-3/3.5/4, Vicuna, and LLaMA-2 on commonsense, arithmetic, symbolic, ethics, and professional knowledge tasks. They decompose methods into prompting, sampling, and aggregation and find that token-level logprob (white-box) aggregation outperforms black-box (verbalized confidence) methods, while consistency-based aggregation offers the best black-box signal. The paper directly compares token entropy, verbalized confidence, and multi-sample consistency as failure-prediction signals.

## Key Claims

1. Token-level logprob (white-box) methods achieve AUROC ~0.605 for failure prediction vs. ~0.522 for single-sample verbalized confidence — **a ~8pp gain from using logprobs vs. text-only features**, directly quantifying the advantage HYP-001 expects.
2. LLMs are systematically overconfident when asked to verbalize confidence; logprob-based entropy is better calibrated but still imperfect.
3. No single uncertainty method wins universally across all task types; professional-knowledge tasks (MMLU) remain hardest for all methods.
4. Combining token logprob entropy with consistency-based aggregation (multi-sample) yields the strongest signal — suggesting HYP-001's entropy approach could be augmented with consistency checks.
5. The logprob–accuracy correlation is weaker for code generation (HumanEval) than for factual QA (TriviaQA) — an important caveat for HYP-001's multi-task evaluation.

## Relevance to HYP-001

This is the most directly relevant empirical baseline for HYP-001's core claim. The ~8pp AUROC advantage of logprob entropy over verbalized confidence (a text-feature proxy) aligns with HYP-001's predicted 5pp improvement in quality_at_budget_normalized. The finding that logprobs are weaker on code tasks (HumanEval) is a concrete risk — HYP-001 should analyze routing accuracy separately per task type. The paper also flags MMLU-professional as hardest, consistent with HYP-001's multi-dataset experimental design covering hard knowledge tasks.

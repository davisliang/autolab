## EXP-001 Experiment Result: Multi-Head 4D vs Single-Score Baseline

### Status: **FAIL**

HYP-002 predicted ≥3pp routing accuracy gain for the multi-head 4D router over
the single-score baseline. The experiment showed the opposite: the baseline
outperformed proposed by **+11.7pp on routing_accuracy** (0.913 vs 0.793).

### Full Metrics (mean ± stddev across seeds 42, 123, 456)

| Metric | Proposed | Baseline | Δ |
|--------|----------|----------|---|
| `routing_accuracy` | 0.793 ± 0.023 | 0.913 ± 0.002 | **−0.120** |
| `cost_savings_ratio` | 0.744 ± 0.009 | 0.716 ± 0.013 | +0.029 |
| `quality_at_matched_cost` | 0.955 ± 0.005 | 0.984 ± 0.002 | **−0.029** |

### Per-seed breakdown

**Baseline routing_accuracy**: [0.911, 0.911, 0.916]
**Proposed routing_accuracy**: [0.760, 0.804, 0.813]

### Sanity gate

Passed: initial_loss=1.099, final_loss=0.137, drop=87.5% (threshold: ≥50%).

### Failure analysis

1. **Heuristic dimension labels add noise, not signal.** The 4D labeller uses
   keyword/regex rules that imperfectly identify RD/DS/AMB/CR dimensions.
   These labels introduce label noise in the intermediate heads, which
   propagates into the meta-features fed to the Random Forest. The baseline
   direct LR bypasses this noisy intermediate step.

2. **MiniLM embeddings already encode complexity implicitly.** The 384-dim
   embedding space from all-MiniLM-L6-v2 captures semantic similarity
   including domain and task type. The direct 3-class LR over these embeddings
   learns the relevant routing signal end-to-end without the bottleneck of
   4 binary proxies.

3. **Two-stage training induces error accumulation.** The proposed router's
   meta-features are head *probabilities* (not ground truth), so the RF
   learns on imperfect inputs. With only 1050 training examples (70% of 1500),
   this amplifies variance.

4. **Proposed achieves slightly higher cost savings (+2.9pp)** because it
   routes more aggressively to haiku/sonnet even when oracle says opus is
   needed — a precision-cost tradeoff, not a quality win.

### Implication

The hypothesis that structured dimensional decomposition improves routing over
single-score classifiers is **not supported** under this experimental setup.
The null result is informative: rich embeddings are expressive enough that
handcrafted feature decomposition adds overhead without gain. A follow-up could
test whether *jointly learned* dimension heads (optimized with the routing
objective end-to-end) reverse this result.

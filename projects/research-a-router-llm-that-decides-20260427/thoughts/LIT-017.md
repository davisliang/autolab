## Summary

Kuhn, Gal, and Farquhar (2023) introduce semantic entropy — uncertainty measured over clustered meanings rather than raw token sequences. They show that token-level predictive entropy (the signal HYP-001 proposes) is a valid but imperfect proxy because semantically equivalent sentences (e.g., "Paris is France's capital" vs. "France's capital is Paris") have the same meaning but different token distributions, artificially inflating entropy. Semantic entropy corrects this by NLI-based clustering before computing entropy. Semantic entropy significantly outperforms token-level entropy and P(True) baselines on TriviaQA and CoQA.

## Key Claims

1. Token-level predictive entropy suffers from the semantic equivalence problem: the model may be uncertain *lexically* while being certain about the *meaning*, causing false escalations in entropy-based routing.
2. Semantic entropy (entropy over NLI-clustered meaning groups) outperforms standard predictive entropy at predicting model accuracy: ~8pp AUROC improvement on TriviaQA.
3. Semantic entropy is unsupervised and requires no auxiliary labels or fine-tuning — only a model capable of NLI entailment judgments.
4. Both token-level entropy and semantic entropy scale better with model size; smaller models show less reliable calibration with either measure.

## Relevance to HYP-001

This paper provides the key limitation of HYP-001's proposed mechanism: naive token-level entropy may be noisy due to lexical ambiguity. Two mitigations follow: (a) using response-level entropy aggregates (mean/max over tokens) rather than sequence-level log-prob, and (b) considering semantic entropy as a stronger signal variant. The ~8pp AUROC gap between semantic and token-level entropy is a direct risk factor for HYP-001's predicted 5pp improvement over text-feature routers — if semantic entropy is needed to beat RouteLLM, the overhead (requiring NLI calls) may negate the efficiency advantage.

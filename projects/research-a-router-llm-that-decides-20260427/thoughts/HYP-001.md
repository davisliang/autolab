## Motivation

Most existing routers (FrugalGPT, RouteLLM) route using input-side features: query length, keyword heuristics, or embedding similarity to training examples labeled by difficulty. These are indirect proxies for what we actually care about: whether the small model will produce a good answer.

A more direct signal is the small model's *own uncertainty* about its response. If Haiku's output token distribution is highly peaked (low entropy), it is confident — keep the answer. If it is diffuse (high entropy), it is unsure — escalate to Sonnet or Opus.

## Mechanism

1. Send every query to Haiku and collect log-probabilities.
2. Compute per-token entropy H(t) = -sum p log p; aggregate to response-level entropy (mean or max over tokens).
3. Train a lightweight threshold (or a 2-layer MLP over [H_mean, H_max, H_first_token]) on a labeled calibration set that maps entropy to escalation decisions.
4. Route to Sonnet if escalation triggered once; route to Opus if the Sonnet response also has high entropy (cascade).

## Why this beats input-side routing

- Input features are task-type proxies; entropy is a direct readout of model uncertainty for *this specific instance*.
- Entropy generalizes to query distributions unseen at router training time.
- The overhead is one Haiku forward pass (already cheap); no separate embedding model needed.

## Experimental Setup

- Dataset: MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, TruthfulQA (diverse coverage of difficulty and type)
- Baseline: RouteLLM (text-feature SVM) at matched average-cost budget points
- Metric: quality_at_budget_normalized = quality_score / (api_cost / reference_cost), averaged across 5 budget points (10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100% of all-Opus cost)
- Seeds: [42, 123, 456]
- Expected effect size: ≥5pp improvement in quality_at_budget_normalized vs. text-feature baseline

## Open Questions

- Does entropy correlate with actual quality degradation uniformly across task types, or only for knowledge-retrieval tasks?
- What is the overhead of Haiku log-prob collection (API pricing for logprobs)?

## Summary

Kadavath et al. (2022, Anthropic) study whether language models can assess the validity of their own outputs. They show large models are well-calibrated on multiple-choice questions, and that output-level self-evaluation via P(True) — asking the model if its sampled answer is correct — distinguishes correct from incorrect samples. They also train P(IK) ("probability I know") probes that generalize partially across task types. Appendix B explicitly studies entropy and loss as discriminants of model knowledge.

## Key Claims

1. Large models are well-calibrated on multiple-choice questions; token-level probabilities assigned to answer tokens correlate closely with empirical accuracy (Brier score improves with scale).
2. P(True) self-evaluation works without fine-tuning: a model can estimate whether its own sampled answer is correct, with AUROC ~0.65–0.75 on TriviaQA and HumanEval.
3. Appendix B shows that *entropy of output token distribution* is predictive of correctness, though P(True) outperforms raw entropy on several tasks.
4. P(IK) probes trained on TriviaQA generalize to GSM8k and HumanEval, suggesting uncertainty signals transfer across domains.
5. RLHF-trained models appear miscalibrated but temperature-tuned become well-calibrated — important for using Claude Haiku (RLHF-trained) as the routing oracle.

## Relevance to HYP-001

This paper provides foundational justification for entropy as a routing signal. The Appendix B result that entropy is predictive of correctness (though weaker than P(True)) directly supports HYP-001's mechanism. The calibration gap between RLHF and base models (point 5) is an important confound: Claude Haiku is RLHF-tuned, which may require temperature calibration before using its entropy for routing. This is a practical prerequisite HYP-001's experimental design should address.

## Summary

Zhang et al. (NAACL 2024) show that standard instruction-tuned LLMs are trained to always complete sentences, which causes them to fabricate answers (and express false confidence) on questions beyond their parametric knowledge. R-Tuning addresses this by identifying the gap between parametric knowledge and instruction tuning data, then constructing a refusal-aware dataset that teaches models to append "I am unsure" to answers for out-of-knowledge questions. Refusal ability transfers as a meta-skill to unseen domains. Learning uncertainty during training improves calibration more than applying uncertainty filtering at test time.

## Key Claims

1. **Hallucination from forced completion**: Standard fine-tuning forces models to always produce an answer, causing overconfident wrong responses on out-of-parametric-knowledge questions — the root cause of verbalized confidence miscalibration.
2. **R-Tuning fixes calibration**: Models fine-tuned with refusal-aware data express uncertainty on truly unknown questions, improving both calibration ECE and accuracy on answered questions simultaneously.
3. **Meta-skill transferability**: Refusal ability learned on in-domain tasks transfers to out-of-domain tasks, showing calibration is a domain-agnostic capacity.
4. **Training > test-time filtering**: Learning uncertainty during training outperforms applying uncertainty-based test filters, suggesting Platt scaling on top of poorly-calibrated base signals has a ceiling.

## Relevance to HYP-004

HYP-004 uses Haiku's self-reported confidence (1–10 scale via prompt elicitation) as the routing signal, then applies Platt scaling to calibrate it. This paper reveals a critical threat to HYP-004:
- Haiku, like all instruction-tuned LLMs, is trained to always produce confident answers — its verbalized 1-10 confidence likely overestimates true correctness probability on hard/unknown questions
- Platt scaling is a monotone transformation — it cannot fix systematic overconfidence on the hardest queries that need escalation the most
- This may explain why confidence-based cascades often underperform trained routers: the signal is structurally biased, not just miscalibrated in scale
- **Mitigation path**: Use self-consistency (multiple samples, majority vote) instead of verbalized confidence — LIT-026 shows this is better calibrated

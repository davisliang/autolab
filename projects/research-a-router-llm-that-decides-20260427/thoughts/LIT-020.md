## Summary

Lin et al. (2021) introduce TruthfulQA: 817 adversarially-designed questions spanning 38 categories where models tend to produce confident, plausible-sounding but false answers (imitative falsehoods learned from training data). The key finding is that larger models are *less* truthful, not more, making the benchmark ideal for testing the "confidently wrong" failure mode central to HYP-005.

## Key Claims

1. The best GPT-3 model achieved only 58% truthfulness vs. 94% for humans on TruthfulQA, with larger models performing worse (more imitative falsehoods from richer training distributions).
2. Models generate false answers that mimic popular human misconceptions, meaning high per-token confidence doesn't correlate with truthfulness here — invalidating entropy-only routing signals.
3. Performance on TruthfulQA does not improve with model scale alone; fine-tuning toward truthfulness is required.
4. False answers tend to be fluent and confident — this is exactly the "confidently wrong" scenario that HYP-005's divergence signal is designed to catch where entropy routing would fail.

## Relevance to HYP-005

TruthfulQA is HYP-005's primary evaluation dataset, chosen specifically because it exhibits the "confidently wrong" failure mode that entropy routing cannot detect. This paper validates that assumption: GPT-3 is confident but wrong on these questions (high per-token probability on false answers). A divergence-based router should trigger more escalations on TruthfulQA than a pure entropy router, producing a measurable gap in quality_at_budget_normalized on this benchmark.

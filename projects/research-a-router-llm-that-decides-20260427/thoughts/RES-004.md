## Result Analysis: EXP-004

### Outcome: FAIL

Thompson Sampling (TS) does **not** achieve ≥0.08 improvement over the static logistic regression baseline on OOD quality-cost AUC. In fact, TS is on average **worse** by 0.038 AUC points.

### Per-seed Results

| Seed | AUC_OOD TS | AUC_OOD Static | Delta | Notes |
|------|-----------|----------------|-------|-------|
| 42   | 0.3610    | 0.4543         | -0.093 | TS over-exploits Opus (avg_cost=0.96), high cost drag |
| 123  | 0.4899    | 0.4613         | +0.029 | Only seed where TS beats static |
| 7    | 0.4616    | 0.4522         | +0.010 | Marginal positive |
| 2024 | 0.3182    | 0.4625         | -0.144 | Worst case: TS wildly over-exploits |
| 99   | 0.4743    | 0.4640         | +0.010 | Marginal positive |
| **Mean** | **0.4210** | **0.4589** | **-0.038 ± 0.068** | — |

### Why TS Fails

1. **Cold-start cost on OOD**: LinTS with d=32 needs O(d²) updates to concentrate its posterior. With only 500 OOD queries, the bandit hasn't converged — it oscillates between exploration and exploitation without stable routing.

2. **Over-exploitation in low-signal contexts**: On seeds where TS happens to see Opus work well early (seed 42, 2024), it locks onto Opus for nearly all 500 OOD queries (avg_cost = 0.96 normalized). This inflates cost without proportional quality gain since the reward function penalizes cost.

3. **Static baseline advantage**: The logistic regression trained on 2000 ID examples (with calibrated labels) learns the expected-reward ranking well enough that it routes to Sonnet/Opus at a cost-efficient rate. It's simple and robust.

4. **AUC metric penalizes high cost**: The quality-cost AUC curve counts quality only at cost ≤ threshold. Expensive Opus routing at the threshold boundary hurts TS's AUC.

### In-Distribution Comparison

TS consistently outperforms static on ID (mean AUC_ID: TS=0.563 vs static=0.503), suggesting the bandit is learning the reward structure but exploiting to the wrong side of the cost-quality tradeoff when transitioning to OOD.

### Implication for HYP-006

The cross-domain transplant of contextual bandits into LLM routing does not deliver the predicted benefit in this simulated setting. The core failure mode is the cold-start problem: 500 online OOD queries are insufficient for a 32-dim LinTS to adapt its policy when starting from uninformative priors. A lighter-weight context encoding (e.g., topic cluster membership, d≤8) or warm-start initialization from static router weights would likely improve OOD adaptation speed.

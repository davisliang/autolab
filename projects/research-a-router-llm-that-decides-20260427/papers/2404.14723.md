###### Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown remarkable performance across various tasks. Previous methods, such as Reinforcement Learning (RL) from Human Feedback (RLHF) through online RL algorithms, have been pivotal. However, effectively aligning with human preferences remains a significant challenge. Recently, offline approaches like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) have emerged as promising alternatives. Nonetheless, several limitations hinder the utilization of these alignment methods: 1) Strong reliance on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), 2) Time and cost intensiveness of ranking preferences, 3) Risks of overfitting, and 4) Inefficient learning processes. Addressing these limitations is crucial for advancing the effectiveness and applicability of alignment methods in LLMs. Several versions of DPO have been introduced to mitigate its shortcomings. However, a comprehensive comparison with its variants in addressing these issues is still lacking. Moreover, questions persist regarding the ideal dataset size for training these methods and the most effective sampling and ranking strategies for preferences. In this study, we aim to fill these gaps by investigating the performance of DPO and its variants on the MT-Bench that incorporates 8 different domains. (See Figure[1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants").) Additionally, we explore the influence of data’s quantity and quality on the efficacy of alignment methods. Overall, our study identifies which alignment methods do better/worse in which domains. We anticipate that our findings will lead to additional research into crafting more robust models that address alignment challenges.

1 Introduction
--------------

LLMs have sparked a revolution in tackling real-world challenges, showcasing impressive abilities across diverse domains requiring reasoning and specialized knowledge. These models excel in mathematical reasoning/problem-solving ps1; ps2; ps3, code generation/programming prog2; prog3; prog1, text generation tg1; tg2, summarization, and creative writing, among other tasks. Notably, LLMs have achieved significant alignment with human preferences, with alignment methods including Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) sanh2022multitask; ouyang2022training. While RLHF (after SFT) exhibits remarkable performance compared to just SFT, it faces limitations such as reward hacking liu2024statistical. Therefore, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) rafailov2023direct, a cutting-edge offline reinforcement learning method, has been proposed to optimize human preferences without the need for the RL process.

Recent studies have highlighted limitations in alignment methods, including issues like overfitting, inefficient learning and memory utilization, preferences ranking, and dependence on preferences across various scenarios like dialogue systems tunstall2023zephyr, summarization, sentiment analysis wu2023pairwise, helpful and harmful question answering liu2024statistical, and machine translation xu2024contrastive. Despite the significance of these studies, none have thoroughly examined critical ambiguities in alignment, such as the learnability of emerged alignment methods without SFT, fair comparison between these methods, evaluating their performance post-SFT, the impact of data volume on performance, and weaknesses inherent in these methods. Addressing these areas is crucial for gaining a comprehensive understanding, as they elucidate alternative viewpoints or the absence thereof, enabling a more precise and nuanced interpretation. Moreover, they play a pivotal role in language reasoning and inference.

In this study, we explore alignment methods and assess their performance across eight domains. We delve into two key scenarios: (A) Aligning with and without SFT and (B) Assessing the impact of data quality and quantity. In scenario A, we scrutinize alignment method performance pre and post-SFT. In scenario B, we evaluate the effectiveness of the DPO method across various sizes of training datasets. Additionally, we introduce a novel approach called Preferences Pruning (PP), which involves finding the best setup to generate chosen and rejected preferences via the SFT model. This allows us to showcase the DPO method’s performance across diverse, high-quality datasets. We evaluate our models on MT-Bench and different benchmarks in the Open LLM Leaderboard.

This study reveals that certain alignment methods, such as Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO) ethayarajh2023halos and Identity Preference Optimization (IPO) azar2023general, comparable performance even without the need for SFT. However, we also uncover weaknesses in alignment methods concerning mathematical operations, reasoning, mathematics, and coding tasks (Figure [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants")). Conversely, variants of the DPO method demonstrate enhanced performance in areas like writing, content extraction, and knowledge-based queries yet falter in mathematical, reasoning, and coding domains. Notably, we find that some alignment methods can match or surpass GPT-4’s performance in certain domains (See Figure [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S2.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 2 Related Works ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants")), underscoring their potential for generalization. Furthermore, we observe that DPO can achieve comparable performance even with limited datasets. Additionally, our comparison of various high-quality datasets highlights the impressive performance of datasets generated by state-of-the-art (SOTA) models compared to those produced by SFT models.

In summary, our contributions are as follows:

1.   1.We explore the learning capabilities of alignment methods, aiming to mitigate overfitting challenges within the DPO framework. Our findings indicate that IPO and KTO show performance on par with SFT and are competitive with SOTA models (See Figure [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants")). 
2.   2.We extensively examine the effectiveness of alignment methods across eight distinct domains, uncovering their limitations in certain areas, particularly in tasks requiring reasoning. 
3.   3.Additionally, we introduce a statistical approach to ranking preferences, termed Preference Pruning (PP), aimed at determining the good configuration for generating chosen and rejected responses via the SFT model. We also show that BLEU score and ROUGE value for different number of generation on each temperature is in the specefic boundary (See Figure [4](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S2.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 2 Related Works ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants")). 
4.   4.We devise a scenario to assess the performance of DPO across varying sizes of training datasets, demonstrating the sensitivity of DPO performance to both data quality and quantity.

2 Related Works
---------------

Recent advancements in pre-training LLMs, such as LLaMA-2 tg2, GPT-3 gpt3, Gopher rae2022scaling, Vicunna vicuna2023, Mistral jiang2023mistral, and PaLM 2 anil2023palm, have led to impressive performance gains in zero-shot radford2019language and few-shot chowdhery2022palm scenarios across various tasks. However, when applied to downstream tasks, LLMs’ performance tends to degrade. While fine-tuning models using human completions aids in alignment and performance enhancement, obtaining human preferences for responses is often more feasible than collecting expert demonstrations. Consequently, recent research has shifted focus towards training LLMs using human preferences. In this section, we present a brief review of various tasks and settings where LLMs have been shown the performance on alignment.

RLHF christiano2023deep proposed to optimize for maximum reward operates by engaging with a reward model trained using the Bradley-Terry (BT) model bong2022generalized through reinforcement algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) schulman2017proximal. While RLHF enhances model performance, it grapples with challenges such as instability, reward hacking, and scalability inherent in reinforcement learning.

Recent studies have introduced methods to address these challenges by optimizing relative preferences without depending on reinforcement learning (RL). Optimizing a model using the BT model on preference datasets helps ensure alignment with human preferences.

Sequence Likelihood Calibration (SLiC) zhao2023slichf introduced a novel approach to ranking preferences produced by a supervised fine-tuned (SFT) model, employing calibration loss and regularization fine-tuning loss during training. Meanwhile, Rank Response with Human Feedback (RRHF) yuan2023rrhf trains the SFT model utilizing a zero-margin likelihood contrastive loss, assuming multiple ranked responses for each input. Despite their efficacy, SLiC and RRHF lack theoretical underpinnings. In response, DPO proposed a method to fit an SFT model directly to human preferences using the Bradley-Terry (BT) model, offering theoretical insights into the process.

Statistical Rejection Sampling Optimization (RSO) liu2024statistical combines the methodologies of SLiC and DPO while introducing an enhanced method for gathering preference pairs through statistical rejection sampling. IPO azar2023general, akin to DPO approaches, has mathematically demonstrated the limitations of the DPO approach regarding overfitting and generalization, proposing a comprehensive objective for learning from human preferences. Zephyr tunstall2023zephyr has enhanced DPO by leveraging state-of-the-art (SOTA) models to generate responses for the same input and ranking them using teacher models like GPT-4. Additionally, they highlight the necessity of SFT as a preliminary step before employing DPO.

KTO ethayarajh2023halos, inspired by Kahneman and Tversky’s seminal work on prospect theory KahnemanandTversky, aims to maximize the utility of LLM generations directly rather than maximizing the log-likelihood of preferences. This approach eliminates the need for two preferences for the same input, as it focuses on discerning whether a preference is desirable or undesirable.

Self-Play fIne-tuNing (SPIN) chen2024selfplay introduced a self-training approach to enhance DPO using the dataset employed in the SFT step. The key idea of this approach is to utilize synthetic data generated as the rejected response and the gold response from the SFT dataset as the chosen response. Meanwhile, Constrictive Preference Optimization (CPO) xu2024contrastive proposed an efficient method for learning preferences by combining the maximum-likelihood loss and the DPO loss function, aiming to improve memory and learning efficiency.

We note that the aforementioned works lack comparative studies on alignment methods concerning both completion and preference learning. While these studies address unlearning a DPO method without the SFT step, further exploration of alternative methods is warranted. Although the significance of high-quality preferences is widely acknowledged, there remains a necessity to explore the influence of data quantity on DPO performance. Gathering and ranking preferences are costly endeavors, underscoring the potential of approaches like SPIN and RSO to address this issue. Additionally, the crucial aspect of generalization remains unexplored. While aligning a model aims to enhance performance across all categories, improving alignment methods often comes at the expense of performance in other areas. Further investigation in this regard is necessary. To this end, we examine the performance of alignment methods both before and after SFT to assess the learning capabilities of DPO variants. Moreover, we highlight the weaknesses of alignment methods by comparing their performance across eight different domains, demonstrating the significant impact of dataset quantity and quality on performance. 3 Analysis Scenarios
--------------------

In this section, we outline two evaluation scenarios aimed at comparing the performance of alignment methods in two distinct configurations: 1) single-turn and 2) multi-turn conversations. Further elaboration is provided in Section 4.

### 3.1 Scenario A: Aligning with and without SFT

In this scenario, a model fine-tuned on a dataset using DPO is needed to generate responses to user questions in various domains, such as reasoning, mathematics, programming/coding, extraction, roleplay, and writing. Initially, the model should align with the preferences dataset. In the second part of the scenario, a model without any fine-tuning trains with alignment methods.

#### Motivation:

This scenario is significant because it explores streamlining the alignment process to enhance its efficiency potentially. By omitting certain steps like SFT, the alignment process may be accelerated. The objective here is to assess the performance of various DPO variants in comparison to the SFT model. The motivation behind this scenario is to address the following inquiries: 1) What level of performance enhancement do different alignment methods achieve post-SFT? 2) Does learning occur following the training of a pre-trained model with alignment methods? 3) Which alignment method generally outperforms others in both single-turn and multi-turn scenarios? 4) How does the improvement in performance influence different domains?

### 3.2 Scenario B: Quality and Quantity of Data

In this scenario, our focus is solely on DPO, investigating its performance across varying amounts of training data. Additionally, we introduce a statistical method to select preferences generated by the SFT model to facilitate comparison with other high-quality datasets. As in the previous scenario, a model generates responses to user questions across six different domains.

#### Motivation:

In this scenario, the absence of preference datasets poses a significant challenge to alignment. Real-world applications often lack such datasets, and generating preferences using the SFT model is ambiguous due to the variability in responses for the same input. Additionally, employing human or teacher models like GPT-4 for ranking responses incurs high costs. Thus, it is crucial to address the following questions: 1) Which is superior between generating responses from the SFT model and utilizing state-of-the-art models? 2) Is human feedback indispensable for ranking responses, or is artificial intelligence feedback (AIF) sufficient? 3) What impact do the quality and quantity of training have on the performance of the assigned model?

#### Preference Pruning (PP):

We adhere to the hypothesis within the RSO that improving the SFT ranking of the SFT generation can enhance the DPO’s performance. Therefore, we introduce a Preference Pruning (PP) approach aimed at selecting the sub-optimal configuration for generating both chosen and rejected preferences. Our hypothesis suggests that to identify the optimal configurations, extensive data generation on various configurations followed by evaluation may not be necessary.

An intriguing discovery reveals that the BLEU score and ROUGE-L metrics for various configurations fall within specific ranges. Leveraging this insight, rather than generating extensive data for evaluation, we opt to create a small subset of each configuration. Our hypothesis posits that by analyzing the BLEU score and ROUGE-L metrics for this subset of data, we can effectively prune configurations and identify a sub-optimal setup. To test this hypothesis, we randomly select a small batch of data for each configuration, generate the response, and compute the BLEU score and ROUGE-L metrics. This process is repeated 10 times for each configuration. The resulting data, as depicted in Figure [4](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S2.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 2 Related Works ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants"), validates our hypothesis. Notably, we observe that the BLEU score and ROUGE-L metrics exhibit variation for the same input on different configurations. Furthermore, we observe a decrease in ROUGE-L with increasing temperature, while the BLEU score remains relatively consistent within an acceptable range. However, it’s worth noting that the range of BLEU scores, such as at temperature 0.8, can be extensive.

The objective is to identify a temperature parameter that yields preferences closely resembling chosen responses within the datasets while selecting a temperature that results in low similarity with chosen responses as rejected responses. In general, the BLEU score evaluates the generated response against a reference text, assigning a score based on the word overlap between the two texts, while ROUGE measures the n-gram overlap between the generated and reference responses. Our hypothesis suggests that a sub-optimal configuration for chosen responses will exhibit high BLEU score and ROUGE-L values, as we anticipate the chosen responses to mirror the reference response closely. Conversely, an optimal configuration for rejected responses will yield low BLEU score and ROUGE-L values, as we prioritize rejected responses with minimal similarity to the references.

Overall, we explore various configurations to identify the sub-optimal settings for generating chosen and rejected responses using the SFT model. We posit that the PP method, coupled with straightforward analysis, effectively addresses the challenge of preference generation. In Section 4.3, we present a comparison of the performance of the PP method and baseline dataset on DPO. Additionally, we treat the chosen preferences as references in the UltraFeedback dataset to calculate the metrics. We anticipate that our approach will demonstrate superior performance on other datasets with more pronounced chosen preferences.

4 Experiments
-------------

### 4.1 Experimental Setup

#### Models:

We conduct all our experiments using zephyr-sft-full and Mistral-7B0instruct-v0.2 which is the one of the current state-of-the-art at the 7B parameter scale. We employed the Transformer Reinforcement Learning (TRL) library for fine-tuning vonwerra2022trl. All models underwent training with the AdamW optimizer and were devoid of weight decay. Additionally, parameter-efficient techniques like LoRA hu2021lora were not utilized. The experiments were conducted on 6 A100 GPUs, employing bfloat16 precision, and typically required 20-24 hours to finish.

#### Datasets:

In this study, we utilize two dialogue datasets: 1) UltraChat ding2023enhancing and 2) UltraFeedback-binarized tunstall2023zephyr. UltraChat comprises 200k examples generated by GPT-3.5-TURBO across 30 topics and 20 text material types, providing a high-quality dataset. This dataset is employed for training the SFT model. Meanwhile, UltraFeedback-binarized consists of 63k pairs of chosen and rejected responses corresponding to a given input and is utilized for training alignment models.

#### Evaluation Metric:

We assess our models in both single-turn and multi-turn scenarios using chat benchmarks. These benchmarks gauge the models’ proficiency in understanding instructions and responding to diverse prompts across various domains. We employ MT-Bench zheng2023judging, which comprises 160 questions spanning eight knowledge domains for evaluation. In MT-Bench, models are tasked with answering a question and subsequently addressing a predefined follow-up question. Model responses are rated on a scale of 1 to 10 by GPT-4, and the final score is calculated as the mean over the two turns. Our evaluation objective is to measure the chat quality of aligned models. Additionally, we also assess models on the Open LLM Leaderboard open-llm-leaderboard, which compare the performance of LLMs across four multiclass classification tasks: ARC clark2018think, HellaSwag zellers2019hellaswag, MMLU hendrycks2021measuring, and Truthful QA lin2022truthfulqa. We also evaluate more our models on lin2022truthfulqa, GSM8k cobbe2021training which is a math problem solving task, and Winogrande sakaguchi2019winogrande which is a commonsense reasoning. While the benchmarks in the Leaderboard does not directly evaluate the conversational quality of chat models, it offers a valuable metric to verify whether fine-tuning has led to regressions in the base model’s reasoning and truthfulness capabilities.

### 5.1 Analysis of Scenario A

#### DPO is strong but not quite.

The analysis of Table [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S4.T1 "Table 1 ‣ Evaluation Metric: ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants") yields several notable insights. Firstly, across all alignment methods, including DPO, KTO, IPO, and CPO, a discernible pattern of learnability emerges. Secondly, while DPO generally outperforms other alignment methods on average, a closer examination reveals an interesting trend. Despite DPO’s overall superiority, KTO consistently achieves a higher score in the first turn. This suggests that while KTO may encounter difficulties with the second question, its initial responses tend to surpass those of other methods, including DPO. Lastly, delving into the specifics of DPO’s performance across different domains, as depicted in Figure [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants"), highlights its comparative weaknesses in mathematics, extraction, STEM, and writing.

#### Alignment methods improve academic task performance.

Tables [4](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S4.T4 "Table 4 ‣ Detail of Training: ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants") and [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S4.T3 "Table 3 ‣ Detail of Training: ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants") present the primary chat results, highlighting the performance comparison of the alignment methods with various other closed-source and open-source LLMs. The findings indicate that KTO outperforms other models in scenarios without SFT. Additionally, we note a greater disparity between the SFT model and alignment models in scenarios where SFT is not utilized compared to cases where SFT is employed prior to alignment.

#### Alignment methods tend to struggle with reasoning.

All alignment methods examined in the study aimed to enhance DPO’s performance. However, as depicted in Figures [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S0.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants") and [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants"), all of them demonstrate shortcomings in reasoning. This suggests that despite advancements in mimicking human-like abilities, the capacity for reasoning appears to diminish.

#### Is the SFT required for other alignment methods?

The Zephyr study indicates the necessity of SFT for DPO. However, the primary question remains whether SFT is indispensable for other alignment methods. To address this query, we outline two experiments.

*   •Aligning-SFT: In this experiment, we train the alignment methods using the Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 pre-trained model. 
*   •SFT: In this experiment, we perform supervised fine-tuning on the Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 model. 

The findings presented in Table [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S4.T2 "Table 2 ‣ Evaluation Metric: ‣ 4.1 Experimental Setup ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants") indicate that only the CPO method exhibits a failure in learning, with the worst score observed after training. Interestingly, both KTO and IPO outperform the SFT model on average. Particularly noteworthy is KTO’s superior performance in addressing the subsequent question. This discovery suggests that the SFT model not only fails to significantly enhance performance but may also impair certain abilities of the model, such as accurately following the question. We refer readers to Appendix [B](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#A2 "Appendix B More Exploration on Alignment+SFT, Alignment-SFT, and PP ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants") and [C](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#A3 "Appendix C Explore Examples ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants") to see more results for alignment methods without SFT.

#### Same or higher than GPT-4.

We noted that while improving overall performance, there is a decrease in the model’s ability in certain domains. However, another intriguing discovery is that not only does IPO achieve an equal score with GPT-4 in Humanities, but CPO also outperforms GPT-4 in the STEM domain (See Figure [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S2.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 2 Related Works ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants")). This finding highlights the alignment methods’ capability to rival state-of-the-art models such as GPT-4.

### 5.2 Analysis of Scenario B

#### Quantity plays a crucial role in alignment methods.

We acknowledge the lack of exploration regarding the quantity of the training set. The primary question revolves around understanding how the size of the training dataset affects the performance of an alignment method. To address this inquiry, we conduct an experiment utilizing the zephyr-sft-full model and train it with DPO on varying training set sizes. We consider training set sizes of 0, 1K, 5K, 10K, and 61K of the training set. Zero represents the performance of the SFT, while 61K denotes the entire dataset in the training set (Figure [6](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#S5.F6 "Figure 6 ‣ The dataset’s quality significantly impacts the performance of alignment methods. ‣ 5.2 Analysis of Scenario B ‣ 5 Results ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants")). The results indicate that DPO’s performance on 5K of the train set outperforms other configurations. An interesting observation is that performance does not necessarily improve with increasing dataset size. This highlights the significance of considering the quantity of training sets in alignment methods. For further exploration, refer to Appendix [B](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14723v2#A2 "Appendix B More Exploration on Alignment+SFT, Alignment-SFT, and PP ‣ All Knowledge You Need about DPO and its Variants").

#### The dataset’s quality significantly impacts the performance of alignment methods.

RSO demonstrated that DPO performance was enhanced by ranking SFT responses and selecting chosen and rejected responses from the ranking. Generally, two approaches are employed to generate a dataset for training a model with alignment methods. The first approach involves generating responses using SFT models, ranking them, and training the model based on the preferences. As exemplified by Zephyr, the second approach utilizes state-of-the-art (SOTA) models to generate responses and ranks them using teacher models such as GPT-4. The primary question is, which approach yields better performance? To address this query, we proposed a method similar to RSO. For further details, refer to Section 3.2. 6 Conclusions
-------------

Aligning models with human preferences poses a significant challenge. Although DPO represents a SOTA offline reinforcement learning method for alignment, it comes with certain limitations, including overfitting and inefficient learning. To address these shortcomings, alternative methods such as DPO have been proposed in recent studies. In this investigation, we raise crucial questions regarding emerging alignment methods. Our findings reveal that, on average, DPO remains stronger than other variants in text completion tasks. However, its variants, such as KTO and IPO without an SFT part, exhibit noteworthy performance. Additionally, we discuss the strengths and weaknesses of alignment methods, emphasizing the need for further attention to reasoning, mathematics, and coding tasks. Moreover, we highlight the significance of training set quantity, demonstrating that DPO can achieve superior performance in certain scenarios with smaller data. Furthermore, we introduce Preferences Pruning (PP) as a statistical method for optimizing the generation of chosen and rejected responses by the SFT model. Through a comparison of DPO performance on dataset generated by SOTA models and PP methods, we illustrate the influence of dataset quality on DPO’s performance. We believe that our study provides valuable insights for researchers interested in AI alignment.

7 Limitations
-------------

The study is subject to several limitations. A key constraint is the challenge of preparing an appropriate dataset for training alignment methods. Furthermore, ranking multiple preferences presents another limitation that can affect the quality of the research. Inefficiencies in learning and memory also hinder progress in alignment research. Additionally, using essential benchmarks like MT-Bench and AlpacaEval dubois2023alpacafarm is costly and necessitates access to GPT-4 for evaluation.

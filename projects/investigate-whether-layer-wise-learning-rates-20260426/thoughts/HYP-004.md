## Cross-domain transplant: PID control theory → layer-wise LR

In classical control, a PID controller outputs a corrective signal u(t) = Kp*e(t) + Ki*∫e(t)dt + Kd*de/dt where e(t) is the error signal. Transplanting to neural network training:

- **Proportional term**: current gradient norm g_l(t) — large norm means the layer is far from a local optimum; increase sensitivity.
- **Integral term**: EMA of g_l over past T steps — persistent high gradient signals the layer is stuck; progressively reduce LR to prevent oscillation.
- **Derivative term**: g_l(t) − g_l(t−1) — rapid gradient increase signals instability; reduce LR sharply.

LR_l(t) = base_lr * (1 + Kp*g_l − Ki*ema_l − Kd*delta_l) clipped to [1e-5, 0.1].

## Why novel

Standard adaptive methods (Adam, RMSProp) apply element-wise second-moment scaling without explicit integral or derivative history at layer granularity. A true layer-level PID with all three terms has not been reported in mainstream optimiser literature.

## Pre-registration

Val accuracy at epoch 10 ≥ Adam (lr=1e-3) baseline + 1.0 pp. Baseline: Adam, lr=1e-3, default betas.
